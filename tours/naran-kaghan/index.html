I Compared the Best Naran-Kaghan Tour Packages for 2026 — Here's What's Worth Booking

By Faisal Zaman· Local, Gilgit-Baltistan· Updated June 2026·14 min read

Naran-Kaghan is the most-booked mountain trip in Pakistan, and for good reason: it's the closest real alpine scenery to the cities, it's cheap, and the road is open all summer. It's also the most over-sold — every other operator runs a Naran package, quality swings wildly, and peak-season crowds can make or break the trip. I grew up in the mountains just north of here and drive this corridor every season, so here's the honest version: what a Naran package should cost in 2026, the itineraries that actually work, and how to book one without ending up in a cramped coaster with a rushed schedule.

I won't push you toward the big agencies already crowding the top of Google. I'd rather give you the real picture and, if you want, arrange the trip myself with people I trust on the ground.

How I compare Naran packages

Value first (what you actually get, not the lowest sticker), then realistic pacing (Saif-ul-Malook and Babusar in one day is a trap), the operator's vehicle and crowd-handling, transparency on what's included, and whether they can get you up to Babusar Pass when it's open. How Naran trips work in 2026

Naran is all by road — there's no airport, and that's part of why it's so affordable. From Islamabad it's roughly 6–7 hours up through Abbottabad, Balakot and the Kaghan Valley. Most packages start in Islamabad; travellers from elsewhere join there.

Two things shape every Naran trip. First, Lake Saif-ul-Malook sits above Naran on a rough track — you switch to a 4WD jeep for the last stretch (a separate, often-haggled cost; a good package includes it). Second, Babusar Top (4,173m), the pass that links Kaghan to Gilgit-Baltistan, is only open once the snow clears — roughly late June to September. If Babusar is your goal, timing matters, and it's also the natural bridge if you want to carry on into Hunza or Skardu.

What a Naran package costs in 2026

Naran is the cheapest mountain trip in Pakistan, so prices start low and rise with comfort. From current operator listings (June 2026): a 2-day quick trip runs around Rs 7,000, a 3-day Naran + Saif-ul-Malook around Rs 10,500–13,000, a 4-day couple trip around Rs 18,500, and 5-day routes adding Babusar and Lulusar around Rs 22,000–25,000. Private trips cost more per head but buy you space, pace and a better vehicle. What moves the price: group size, hotel standard, whether the Saif-ul-Malook jeep is included, and how far up the corridor you go.

What's included — and what's not

A fair Naran package includes transport with a driver, every night's hotel, breakfast, a guide or coordinator, and the local sightseeing. The better ones bundle the Saif-ul-Malook jeep so it isn't haggled on the day. Commonly excluded: the jeep (if not bundled), lunches and dinners, boat rides at the lake, entry to any private resorts, tips, and personal spending. Because Naran is a price-driven market, the cheapest quotes usually cut exactly here — a packed coaster instead of a comfortable vehicle, a hotel "in Kaghan" that's really an hour from Naran, or the jeep left off so the headline looks lower. Ask for the inclusions in writing and check the vehicle and the hotel name specifically; that's where Naran packages quietly differ.

When to go in 2026

Peak summer (June–August) is the main season — everything open, Babusar passable, and the busiest, most crowded time (book hotels early). May & September are the sweet spots: the lower valley and Saif-ul-Malook are open, prices and crowds drop, but Babusar may still be closed in early May or after September snow. October onward the upper roads close and Naran largely shuts down for winter. If Babusar Top is a must, aim for July–early September, its reliable window.

Naran packages from Islamabad, Lahore, Karachi & Multan

From Islamabad is the standard — it's the closest city (6–7 hours) and where packages start. From Lahore, add the motorway leg to Islamabad/Abbottabad (a few hours by coach or car); most join the group on the way. From Karachi or Multan, the sensible route is to fly or take the motorway coach to Islamabad and start from there — I quote these with the connecting leg included so you see one number. Tell me your city and I'll price it door to door.

Is Naran safe and open for tourists in 2026?

Yes on both counts, with the normal mountain caveats. Naran is one of the safest, most family-friendly destinations in Pakistan — it's mainstream domestic tourism, with police and rescue presence in season. The real "is it open?" question is about the roads, not security: the Naran road and Saif-ul-Malook track open as the snow melts (usually May), and Babusar Pass opens roughly late June and closes after the first heavy September snow. Landslides can briefly block sections after heavy rain. So Naran itself is reliably open all summer; only the highest stretches are seasonal. Check the live road & pass status before you travel, and I'll always tell you honestly if conditions are against your dates.

What to check before you book

Five questions sort the good operators from the rest: Is the Saif-ul-Malook jeep included or extra? What vehicle are you actually in (a private car and a 40-seat coaster are very different trips)? Which named hotels? Is Babusar in the plan, and what's the weather back-up if it's closed? And is the price per person, on what sharing basis? Red flags: vague inclusions, no written itinerary, a suspiciously low price (usually a packed coaster and a rushed day), and pressure to pay fast.

Naran on its own, or your gateway into GB?

Naran works as a standalone 3–5 day trip — and it's also the classic road gateway to Gilgit-Baltistan. Once Babusar Top is open, you can carry straight over the pass into Chilas and on to Hunza or Skardu, turning a Naran trip into a full Gilgit-Baltistan circuit. If you have 8–10 days and the pass is open, that combined route is the best value in the country — two regions, one road trip. Tell me and I'll build the through-route.

The lakes and stops worth your time

Naran's pull is its alpine lakes, but they're not all equal and a good package picks the right ones for your days. Lake Saif-ul-Malook is the headline — a glacial lake ringed by peaks, with the Malika Parbat reflection on a still morning. Go early; by midday the jeep track and shoreline are packed. Lake Lulusar, on the road toward Babusar, is bigger, quieter and arguably more beautiful, and it barely adds time if you're heading up the pass anyway. Siri Paye Meadows above Shogran is the best lower-valley stop — rolling green meadows with a mountain backdrop, reached by a short jeep ride, ideal if you're easing in from Islamabad.

For those who can walk, Lake Dudipatsar is the reward — a day hike from Besal into one of the most striking lake basins in the region, far from the crowds. Ansoo Lake (the "tear-shaped" lake) is a tougher trek for fit walkers only. If you just want the iconic shots without trekking, a 3-day trip covering Saif-ul-Malook and a Kaghan drive is plenty; if you want the meadows, lakes and Babusar, give it five.

How to avoid the worst of the crowds

Naran's one genuine downside is how busy it gets — in July and August the bazaar, the Saif-ul-Malook track and the good hotels all fill up, and a badly planned trip becomes a traffic jam with a view. A few things help, and any operator worth booking will build them in. Travel in late May–June or September if your dates are flexible: the valley is open, the light is gentler and the crowds thin out (just check Babusar's status). Start the Saif-ul-Malook jeep early — first light beats both the heat and the queue. Avoid weekends and public holidays, when day-trippers from the cities pour in. And strongly consider a private vehicle over a group coaster: it's the single biggest upgrade to comfort and pace, and for a family or couple the per-head difference is smaller than you'd think. When I plan a Naran trip I time the lake mornings and the Babusar run deliberately around the crowds — it's the difference between a relaxed trip and a stressful one.

FAQ

How much does a Naran-Kaghan tour package cost in 2026?

From current operator listings: about Rs 7,000 for a 2-day trip, Rs 10,500–13,000 for a 3-day Naran + Saif-ul-Malook, around Rs 18,500 for a 4-day couple trip, and Rs 22,000–25,000 for a 5-day route with Babusar and Lulusar — per person, ex-Islamabad, group sharing. Private trips cost more per head. Ask for a current itemised quote.

Is Naran open for tourists in 2026?

Yes. Naran and Lake Saif-ul-Malook open as the snow clears (usually May) and stay open through summer. Babusar Pass is seasonal — roughly late June to September. The upper roads close for winter from around October.

Is Naran safe to travel to?

Yes — it's one of Pakistan's most popular, family-friendly destinations with a strong in-season tourism presence. The main risks are weather and road conditions (landslides after heavy rain), not security. Check the road status before high-altitude stretches.

How many days do I need for Naran-Kaghan?

Three days covers Naran and Saif-ul-Malook; five lets you add Babusar Top, Lulusar and Shogran without rushing. Two days is doable but tight.

Are Naran packages good for families and couples?

Very — it's the most family-friendly mountain trip in Pakistan. For families or couples I usually recommend a private vehicle over a group coaster for the space and flexible pace.

Can I go from Naran into Hunza or Skardu?

Yes, once Babusar Pass is open (roughly July–September) you can cross into Gilgit-Baltistan and continue to Hunza or Skardu — a great combined road trip if you have 8–10 days.

Is the Saif-ul-Malook jeep included?

Not always — the last rough stretch needs a 4WD and is often quoted separately. Confirm it's in your package so there's no surprise on the day.
