Planning a Hunza Honeymoon? The Packages and Stays I'd Pick for 2026

By Faisal Zaman·Local, Gilgit-Baltistan·Updated October 2026

A Hunza honeymoon is one of the most beautiful (and underrated) you can have in Asia: late March to mid-April blossom or October foliage, snow peaks over your room, and very few crowds. I'm from Gilgit-Baltistan, so here's the honest version - the two published packages worth a look, the genuinely romantic places to stay, and how I'd plan it.

There are very few published Hunza honeymoon packages (most are built custom), so I review what exists, then give you the romantic stays and a plan to build your own.

How to plan a Hunza honeymoon

  • Best months: late March-mid April for the famous cherry blossom, or October for golden foliage (peak usually mid-to-late October). Both are the most romantic windows; book early as they are popular.
  • Pay for the view: a Rakaposhi or valley-facing room is the whole point in Hunza - it is worth the upgrade on a honeymoon.
  • Stay in Karimabad: walkable to the forts, cafes and viewpoints, so you are not driving every evening; Duikar (Eagle's Nest) for sunrise.
  • Build your own: most Hunza honeymoons are custom - send me your dates and budget and I will plan a private route and lock in the best rooms.

FAQ

How much does a Hunza honeymoon package cost?

The published private honeymoon packages run about Rs 135,000 (standard) to Rs 160,000 (luxury) for 5 days. Custom trips vary with the hotel and vehicle - get an itemised quote.

What is the best month for a Hunza honeymoon?

Late March to mid-April for cherry blossom, or October for autumn foliage (peak usually mid-to-late October). Both are mild, photogenic and the most romantic times to go.

Where should we stay in Hunza for a honeymoon?

Serena Altit Fort Residence (heritage and quiet) and Eagle's Nest at Duikar (the legendary Rakaposhi sunrise view) are the most romantic; Luxus is the luxury lake resort at Attabad.

How many days do we need?

Five days covers the Hunza honeymoon highlights comfortably; add two or three if you want Khunjerab Pass and upper Hunza at a relaxed pace.
