I Compared the Northern Pakistan Tour Packages You Can Book from Islamabad in 2026

By Faisal Zaman·Local, Gilgit-Baltistan·Updated October 2026·13 min read

Islamabad is the starting line for almost every northern Pakistan trip. Both the mountain flights and the Karakoram Highway begin here, so when operators quote a "tour package," they almost always mean from Islamabad - which makes this the simplest, cheapest city to travel from. I'm from Gilgit-Baltistan and I help people plan these trips; here's the honest 2026 picture of what's on offer from Islamabad, what it costs, and how to choose.

I won't push the big agencies already topping Google - I'd rather show you the real options and arrange the right one for you.

Why start from Islamabad

It's the gateway: 1-hour flights to Gilgit and Skardu, the KKH road north, and in summer the Babusar Pass route to Naran and Hunza. No connecting leg, so prices are the baseline everyone else adds to.

Every northern trip from Islamabad, in detail

Naran-Kaghan (2-5 days, from Rs 11,500). The closest real mountains - about 6-9 hours by road via Abbottabad and Balakot, all on tarmac until the jeep track to Lake Saif-ul-Malook. It's the cheapest, easiest escape from Islamabad and the right call for a weekend or a first taste of the north. In summer it doubles as the gateway to Gilgit-Baltistan over Babusar Pass. Full breakdown in the Naran packages guide.

Hunza (5-7 days, from Rs 17,500). The headline trip - Karimabad's forts, Attabad Lake, the Passu Cones and Khunjerab Pass. From Islamabad you either fly to Gilgit (about an hour) and drive 2.5 hours up, or take the KKH for two scenic days each way. Five days is enough for the highlights; seven if you want Upper Hunza and the China border without rushing. See the Hunza packages guide.

Skardu (6-8 days, from Rs 25,500). Baltistan's lakes, deserts and the Deosai plateau. The Islamabad-Skardu flight is an hour but frequently cancelled, so most trips build in a buffer or drive in via the KKH and Skardu Road. The cheapest route reaches it over Babusar via Naran in summer; premium private tours fly in. See the Skardu packages guide.

The full Hunza + Skardu circuit (8-10 days, from Rs 34,500). The best way to see Gilgit-Baltistan if you have the time - up through Hunza, across to Skardu, and back, two very different regions in one loop. Details in the Gilgit-Baltistan circuit guide.

Fairy Meadows (3-4 days) and Gilgit (4-6 days). Fairy Meadows - the meadow at the foot of Nanga Parbat - is an adventure add-on reached by a famous jeep track and a short hike; it's usually custom-quoted rather than sold as a fixed package (details here). Gilgit city and its valleys (Naltar, Bagrot) make a good base or a Hunza add-on (Gilgit guide).

Which trip should your first one be?

If you have 3-4 days, do Naran - it's the most reward for the least travel from Islamabad. With 5-7 days, go to Hunza; it's the trip people remember. With 8-10 days, do the Hunza + Skardu circuit and see the real range of Gilgit-Baltistan. Only attempt to "see everything" if you have ten days or more - otherwise you'll spend the trip in a vehicle. Couples and families almost always enjoy a private vehicle over a fixed group coaster; the per-head difference is smaller than most people expect, and the comfort and flexibility are worth it on long mountain days.

What to pack & a word on altitude

Even leaving from warm Islamabad, the north is a different climate - pack a warm layer in every season, as nights are cold and the high passes (Khunjerab 4,693m, Babusar 4,173m, Deosai 4,000m+) can be freezing and fiercely bright at once. Take sun protection, comfortable shoes, any personal medicines, and cash (ATMs are scarce north of Gilgit). On the high-pass days, go up slowly, drink more water than feels necessary, and don't plan anything strenuous the same evening. If anyone in your group has a heart or breathing condition, tell me in advance and I'll keep the route gentle.

Getting north from Islamabad - by air and road

By air: Islamabad-Gilgit and Islamabad-Skardu are roughly one-hour flights (stunning, but weather-dependent and often cancelled - always keep a buffer). By road: the Karakoram Highway runs north to Hunza in about 14-20 hours, normally split over two days. Via Babusar (summer): for Naran and Hunza, the scenic route climbs over Babusar Pass - about 6-9 hours just to Naran. Most trips fly one way and drive the other.

What it costs from Islamabad in 2026

Because there's no connecting leg, Islamabad has the lowest entry prices: Naran from ~Rs 11,500, Hunza from ~Rs 17,500, Skardu from ~Rs 25,500, a full Hunza + Skardu circuit from ~Rs 34,500, and private/honeymoon tours Rs 160,000-190,000. Add flights if you fly rather than drive. Watch for "from" prices that are really deposits.

When to go in 2026

Summer (June-August) opens everything, including the high passes, but late July to mid-August brought multi-day monsoon road closures in both 2025 and 2026, so add buffer days then. Spring (April-May) is Hunza blossom but Babusar usually only opens in late May or June. Autumn (late September-October) is gorgeous for colour. Naran and the low valleys are reachable May-October; Hunza's towns are year-round. For the full circuit, June or September-October are the safest bets on the roads.

Is it safe and open in 2026?

Yes - northern Pakistan is among the safest parts of the country for travellers, and Islamabad is the easiest, most connected base. The caveats are access, not security: seasonal passes, weather-dependent flights, and KKH landslides or protest blockades. Foreign visitors should note the UK FCDO advises against all travel on the KKH between Mansehra and Chilas, so they usually go via Naran-Babusar (summer) or fly. FAQ

How much is a northern tour package from Islamabad in 2026?

Naran from ~Rs 11,500, Hunza from ~Rs 17,500, Skardu from ~Rs 25,500, a full Hunza + Skardu circuit from ~Rs 34,500, and private/honeymoon tours Rs 160,000-190,000 - per person, ex-Islamabad, group sharing. Flights extra if you fly.

How long does it take to reach the north from Islamabad?

Naran ~6-9 hours by road; Hunza ~14-20 hours by KKH (two days) or a 1-hour flight to Gilgit plus a short drive; Skardu a 1-hour flight or a two-day drive.

Should I fly or drive from Islamabad?

Flying saves days but is weather-dependent and often cancelled; the road is scenic but long, and monsoon slides (late July to mid-August) can close it for days. Flying one way and driving the other is the usual best choice.

Is travel to the north from Islamabad safe in 2026?

Yes - it's among the safest regions in Pakistan. The real variables are seasonal road/pass access and weather-dependent flights, not security.
