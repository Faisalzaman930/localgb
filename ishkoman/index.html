Ishkoman Valley

A remote valley in far-northwest Ghizer, reaching toward the Wakhan and Chitral borders — the quiet gateway to Karambar Lake, with trout streams, high passes, and almost no visitors.

Overview

One of GB's
Quietest Corners

Ishkoman is a long, sparsely populated valley in Ghizer District, set in the far northwest of Gilgit-Baltistan toward the Afghan Wakhan corridor and the Chitral side of the watershed. It is one of those places that even many Pakistanis only know by name — off the main tourist circuit and all the better for it.

The valley's main town is Chatorkhand, with smaller settlements scattered up the valley including Imit, Phakora and Bilhanz. Communities here are a mix of Khowar- and Wakhi-speaking people, and the way of life is still tied closely to farming, livestock and the rhythm of the short mountain summer.

For travellers, the appeal is simple and honest: green river-fed villages, clear trout streams, and trails that lead toward Karambar Lake and the high Asumbar and Ishkoman passes. There is very little infrastructure, very little traffic, and very little of the staged tourism you find elsewhere. Come for the quiet, not the comforts.

Pair it with the neighbouring Yasin Valley for a deeper Ghizer loop, or start planning the wider trip from our trip planner.

Reality check: This is remote, lightly served country. Carry enough cash from Gilgit, expect patchy mobile signal, and confirm road, fuel and lodging conditions locally before you set out up the valley.
Things To See & Do

What Ishkoman
Is Known For

A short list of genuinely worthwhile things in a valley where the landscape and the calm are the main attraction.

High alpine lake · trek · summer
Karambar Lake Access
Ishkoman is one of the recognised approaches toward Karambar Lake, a striking high-altitude lake on the watershed at the head of the valley system. Reaching it is a serious multi-day trek into very remote terrain — go with a local guide, proper camping gear and a realistic plan. See our dedicated Karambar Lake trek guide and browse all GB treks before committing.
Villages · culture · valley floor
Chatorkhand & Up-Valley Villages
Chatorkhand is the main town and natural base. From here the road and trails thread past Imit, Phakora and Bilhanz, each a small farming settlement of orchards, terraced fields and flat-roofed houses. It is an unhurried, lived-in landscape rather than a sightseeing checklist — the reward is the people and the pace, not monuments.
Trout streams · rivers · summer
Fishing & River Walks
The Ishkoman river and its side streams hold trout, and fishing is a quiet local pastime. If you want to cast a line, ask locally about any permits or seasonal rules and confirm where it is allowed. Even without fishing, the riverside walks between villages are an easy, beautiful way to spend a day.
High passes · advanced trekking · summer only
Asumbar & Ishkoman Passes
The valley is laced with old cross-mountain routes, including the Asumbar and Ishkoman passes, that historically linked these communities with neighbouring valleys. These are demanding, weather-dependent crossings for experienced trekkers with guides and equipment — open only in the high summer window. Treat them as expeditions, not day hikes.
Getting There

How to Reach
Ishkoman

Local tip: Plan Ishkoman as part of a wider Ghizer trip rather than a quick day run from Gilgit. Shared transport up the valley is irregular, so build in flexibility, start early, and always confirm whether vehicles are running that day before you rely on them.
From GilgitHead west into Ghizer via Gahkuch, then continue up toward Chatorkhand and the Ishkoman valley. Reckon on a few hours' drive from Gilgit via Gahkuch; exact times vary with road, season and vehicle, so confirm locally.
By shared transportReach Gahkuch / Ghizer by shared vehicle from Gilgit, then look for onward transport up the valley toward Chatorkhand. Departures are infrequent — ask at the bazaar and be ready to wait or charter.
By private jeepA hired vehicle from Gilgit or Gahkuch is the most reliable way in, especially for the rougher upper sections. Agree the route and price clearly before departure.
Road conditionSurfaces vary from paved to rough, and conditions change with weather and the season. A high-clearance vehicle is the safer choice for the upper valley. Confirm the latest situation locally.
PermitsCarry your CNIC or passport for routine checkposts. As you head into far-border and high-mountain areas, ask locally whether any additional permissions apply for your specific route.
Practical Information

Staying in
Ishkoman

Where to Stay

Accommodation is basic and limited. Expect simple guesthouses and rooms around Chatorkhand and a few of the larger villages, with modest facilities and meals usually cooked on request. Standards and availability change from season to season, so it is wise to ask ahead in Gahkuch or Gilgit and confirm locally rather than assuming a room will be waiting. With permission from landowners, camping is often possible.

Food & Supplies

Meals are home-style and straightforward — dal, rice, chapati, local dairy and seasonal fruit. There is no restaurant scene in the modern sense; you generally eat where you stay. Stock up on any specific provisions in Gilgit or Gahkuch, since village shops carry only the basics.

Best Time to Visit

Ishkoman sits high and gets genuinely cold, so summer is the season to come. The warmer months bring open roads, green valleys and access to the high passes and lakes; outside that window, conditions deteriorate quickly and many routes close.

Summer (peak)The best window. Roads and trails are most likely open, days are pleasant, and the high country is accessible.
Shoulder monthsCooler and quieter, with a real chance of residual snow on passes and changeable weather. Confirm access before travelling.
WinterNot recommended for visitors. Cold, snow and possible road closures; most facilities effectively shut.
Confirm locally: Weather and access in the far northwest can change fast. Check current conditions in Gilgit or Gahkuch and with your guesthouse or guide before heading up.
FAQ

Ishkoman Valley
Questions

Honest answers to the things travellers most often ask about visiting this remote corner of Ghizer.

Where is Ishkoman Valley?

Ishkoman is a remote valley in Ghizer District, in the far northwest of Gilgit-Baltistan, reaching toward the Afghan Wakhan corridor and the Chitral side of the mountains. Its main town is Chatorkhand, with villages such as Imit, Phakora and Bilhanz further up the valley.

How do I get to Ishkoman from Gilgit?

You travel west into Ghizer via Gahkuch, then continue up toward Chatorkhand. It is roughly a few hours' drive from Gilgit via Gahkuch, but exact times vary with the road, season and vehicle, so confirm locally and consider hiring a private jeep for reliability.

What is there to do in Ishkoman?

The valley is mainly about quiet landscapes: village walks, trout streams, and access toward Karambar Lake and the high Asumbar and Ishkoman passes for experienced trekkers. It is an off-beat, authentic destination rather than a place with built-up attractions.

When is the best time to visit Ishkoman?

Summer is the season to visit. The valley is high and cold, so the warmer months offer the best chance of open roads and access to lakes and passes. Outside summer, expect snow, changeable weather and possible closures.

Is there anywhere to stay in Ishkoman?

Yes, but options are basic and limited — mostly simple guesthouses and rooms around Chatorkhand and larger villages, with meals usually cooked on request. Availability changes seasonally, so ask ahead in Gilgit or Gahkuch and confirm locally before relying on a room.
