Overview

Pakistan's Most
Photographed Valley

Hunza is the valley that makes people cancel their flight home. The combination of hanging glaciers, terrace-farmed apricot and cherry orchards, and the sheer scale of Rakaposhi and Ultar Sar overhead creates a landscape that doesn't feel real.

The main town is Karimabad, perched above the Hunza River at 2,438m. From here you can day-trip to Attabad Lake, drive up to Khunjerab Pass (the Chinese border), hike to Eagle's Nest for sunrise, or sit in a tea house and watch the light move across the mountains for four hours without noticing.

Most tourists spend 3–4 days but need at least a week. The valley is compact but the number of side trips — Passu, Gulmit, Shimshal, Hispar — could fill a month.

Getting There

How to Reach
Hunza Valley

Local tip: The NATCO bus from Gilgit to Karimabad leaves at 7am daily (Rs. 350). It's slower than a private jeep but you see everything. Book a seat the night before.
By airFly to Gilgit Airport (GIL) from Islamabad (~45 min, Rs. 8,000–15,000). Flights are weather-dependent — always have a day buffer. Then 3–4h by road to Karimabad.
By road (KKH)The Karakoram Highway from Islamabad to Karimabad takes 16–20h by NATCO bus or private car. Scenic but gruelling. Break the journey at Chilas.
From GilgitShared jeep Rs. 400–600 (3–4h). Private jeep Rs. 5,000–8,000. NATCO bus Rs. 350.
Within HunzaShared jeeps run between Karimabad, Passu, Gulmit, and Sost. Expect Rs. 150–400 per leg. Renting a private jeep for a full day costs Rs. 6,000–10,000.
NOC permitNot required for Karimabad, Attabad Lake, or Khunjerab Pass (CNIC/passport only at border). Required for Shimshal Valley.

What to Eat

Local Food
Worth Trying

Chapshuro

A flat bread stuffed with minced meat and onions, baked on a flat stone. The closest thing to a Hunzai pizza. Get it at the small bakeries off Karimabad's main bazaar — Rs. 150–250.

Diram Phitti

Buckwheat flatbread cooked in walnut oil. Eaten with apricot kernels and butter. One of the oldest foods in the valley and genuinely unlike anything else.

Mulberry Wine

Hunza is one of very few places in Pakistan where local wine is made and sold openly. The mulberry harvest in July produces sweet, low-alcohol wine. Don't expect Bordeaux — expect something unique.

Apricot products

June–July is apricot season. Eat them fresh off the tree, dried, or as jam. The apricot oil is also sold everywhere and worth bringing home.

Best Time to Visit

When to Go

March – AprilCherry & almond blossom. Cold nights, clear days. Fewer tourists. One of the most beautiful times visually.
May – JunePeak trekking season begins. Passes open, wildflowers on glacial moraines. Best all-around time.
July – AugustApricot & mulberry harvest. Warmest weather. Highest tourist numbers. Book guesthouses in advance.
September – OctoberAutumn colours. Cooler, quieter, golden light. Second-best time. Khunjerab Pass closes late October.
November – FebruaryMost roads open but very cold at altitude. Limited guesthouses. Only for serious winter visitors.
