in Hunza

Six experiences worth making the journey for - from a 700-year-old fort to a lake formed by a landslide and a trek to one of Pakistan's highest lakes.

Activities

What to Do
in Hunza

Heritage · 2h

Visit Baltit Fort

700-year-old royal residence of the Mirs of Hunza, restored by Aga Khan Trust. Entry ~Rs.2,000 for foreigners (2026), less for Pakistanis. Guided tour included. The terrace view of Rakaposhi is alone worth the trip.

Lake · ~30km from Karimabad

Boat Trip on Attabad Lake

Turquoise glacial lake formed by the 2010 landslide. Boat hire Rs.500-800/person for 30 minutes. Best early morning for colour intensity.

Viewpoint · 45min hike

Eagle's Nest Sunrise

Hilltop at 2,900m above Karimabad. Sunrise views of Rakaposhi, Lady Finger, Ultar, and the full Hunza Valley. Hike 45min or take a jeep (roughly Rs.1,500-4,000 return).

Photography · ~45-47km north

Passu Cones & Bridge

Six vertical limestone spires to 6,000m. Best viewed from Hussaini suspension bridge over the Hunza River - the most photographed spot in Hunza.

Trekking · 4-6 days

Rush Lake Trek

From Hopar in Nagar (across the valley), a 4-6 day return trek to Rush Lake at 4,694m - one of Pakistan's highest lakes. Hire a guide and agree a written rate in advance. Hisper/Nagar roads were cut by floods for weeks in 2026 - check access.

Culture · Village visit

Altit Old Village

Walk through the preserved stone lanes of old Altit village below Altit Fort. Quieter than Karimabad, more authentic feel, dramatic views over the gorge.

Planning Your Days

How to Fit
It Together

Hunza is bigger and more spread out than first-time visitors expect, so it pays to group activities by location rather than racing back and forth. Karimabad makes the natural base: Baltit Fort, Altit village and the Eagle's Nest viewpoint are all within easy reach, and a relaxed day or two here covers the cultural highlights without much driving.

Attabad Lake and Passu sit further north along the KKH, so it makes sense to combine them into a single Upper Hunza day - a morning boat trip on the lake, then on to the Passu Cones and the Hussaini bridge in the afternoon light. The Rush Lake trek is a different commitment entirely, starting from Hopar in Nagar across the valley and taking 4-6 days return with a guide, so plan it as its own block rather than squeezing it between sightseeing.

Allow at least three or four days in the valley to do it justice. Rushing Hunza in a single overnight is the most common regret travellers mention.

FAQ

Common
Questions

How many days do I need in Hunza?

At least three to four days lets you cover the cultural sights around Karimabad and an Upper Hunza day to Attabad Lake and Passu without rushing. Add more time if you want to trek.

What is the must-do activity in Hunza?

Baltit Fort is the standout - a 700-year-old royal residence restored by the Aga Khan Trust, with a terrace view of Rakaposhi that is worth the trip on its own.

Can I visit Attabad Lake and Passu in one day?

Yes. They sit close together along the KKH north of Karimabad, so a single day with a private jeep can cover an Attabad boat trip and the Passu Cones and Hussaini bridge.

How much does Baltit Fort cost to enter?

Entry is around Rs.2,000 for foreigners in 2026 and less for Pakistanis, with a guided tour included. Confirm on arrival.

Is the Rush Lake trek a day trip?

No. Rush Lake, one of Pakistan's highest lakes at 4,694m, is reached on a 4-6 day return trek from Hopar in Nagar across the valley and needs a local guide.
