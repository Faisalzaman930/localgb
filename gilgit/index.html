Overview

Where All
Roads Meet

Gilgit sits at the junction of three of the world's greatest mountain ranges - the Karakoram, the Hindu Kush, and the western Himalayas. At 1,500m, it's warmer and drier than the valleys to the north, and it's the logistical hub for almost all travel in GB.

Most travellers pass through Gilgit rather than linger - which is a mistake. The old bazaar, the Kargah Buddha rock carving, the polo ground (home of Pakistan's most ferocious polo), and the confluence of the Gilgit and Hunza rivers are all worth a half-day each.

As a base, Gilgit has the best transport links in the region: flights to Islamabad, connections north to Hunza, east toward Skardu, west toward Chitral, and south down the KKH. The airport is compact and the town is walkable.

Practical note: Gilgit has the best banking infrastructure in GB. If you need to withdraw cash or exchange money before heading into the mountains, do it here. ATMs in Hunza and Skardu exist but are unreliable.
What to See

Gilgit's
Highlights

A city that rewards a day of slow exploration more than most guides suggest.

Rock Carving · ~10km from centre

Kargah Buddha

A 7th-century Buddhist figure carved into a cliff face along Kargah Nullah. The carving is 3m tall, cut into orange rock above a stream gorge. Easy 30-minute walk from the road. Free entry. Largely ignored by tourists who don't know it exists.

Bazaar · City Centre

Gilgit Bazaar

The main market runs along the central road. Dried apricots, walnut oil, local embroidery, Chitrali caps, trekking supplies. The fruit sellers along the outer lanes are the best in GB. Morning is busiest and most photogenic.

Sport · October

Shandur Polo & Local Matches

Gilgit's polo ground hosts local matches most weekends, and the big Jashan-e-Azadi tournament runs from mid-October to 1 November at the Shahi Polo Ground. This is not the manicured sport of the south - it's faster, louder, and played without helmets. Free to watch. Ask at your guesthouse for the schedule.

River Walk · 2km

Gilgit-Hunza Confluence

Where the Gilgit River meets the Hunza River, about 5km east of the city. The two rivers meet at a sharp angle between bare rock walls. Best at golden hour. Take a rickshaw or taxi.

Museum · City Centre

Gilgit Museum

Small regional museum with Buddhist-era artefacts, manuscripts, and local cultural exhibits. Worth an hour. Rs. 200 entry. The Gilgit Manuscripts - some of the oldest Sanskrit Buddhist texts in the world - were found near Gilgit in 1931 and are now held mostly in New Delhi (National Archives of India), with others in Srinagar and London.

Day Trip · 2-3h by jeep

Naltar Valley

A forested valley ~40km from Gilgit (2-3h, the last stretch a rough 4×4 track) with three glacial lakes around 3,050-3,150m. In summer: wildflowers and trout fishing. In winter: Pakistan's main ski resort. Locals come here on weekends - follow their lead.

Getting There

How to
Reach Gilgit

By airPIA flies Islamabad (ISB) to Gilgit (GIL) on ATR turboprops, ~50-60 minutes. Fares: about Rs. 19,000-28,000 one way in 2026, higher in peak season (confirm current). The flight is visual-rules only and weather-dependent - cancellations are frequent and often not retried the same day. Always have a road backup plan.
By road (KKH)Roughly 12-16 hours from Islamabad by bus or shared jeep. NATCO and private coaches (e.g. Faisal Movers) depart Rawalpindi daily, roughly Rs. 3,800-6,000 in 2026 (confirm at the counter). The KKH is paved but passes through Kohistan - travel in daylight. The UK FCDO advises against all travel on the KKH between Mansehra and Chilas, and late July-mid August floods closed it for days in 2025 and 2026, so keep buffer days.
From Hunza~95-110km north, about 2-3.5h by shared jeep (Rs. 400-700, confirm locally) or private car (roughly Rs. 5,000-8,000, more for a jeep; confirm).
From Skardu~200-210km via Jaglot, about 4-5h on the Jaglot-Skardu road, plus 1-2h while flood-damaged sections are repaired (shared transport; confirm the fare locally). There are no Gilgit-Skardu flights.
Airport to cityThe airport is 3km from the bazaar. Taxi: Rs. 300-500. Rickshaw: Rs. 150. Most guesthouses offer free pickup if you call ahead.
Staying

Where to
Sleep

BudgetFrom roughly Rs. 3,500/night (2026, confirm). Guesthouses around the bazaar area. Basic but clean. Shared bathrooms at the lower end. Ask for rooms away from the generator.
Mid-rangeRoughly Rs. 4,000-8,000/night for simpler hotels, Rs. 15,000-20,000 for the better ones (2026). Several reliable hotels along the main road with private bathrooms, hot water, and wifi. Gilgit Serena is upper tier, from about Rs. 23,000.
Note on bookingBook in advance June-September. Gilgit fills quickly as a stopover point. Walk-in rates are 20-30% higher than online.
Where to eat: The dhabas on the main bazaar road serve excellent daal, karahi, and chapati - a local dish runs about Rs. 400-700. For sit-down restaurants, Park Hotel's dining room and Madina Restaurant are both reliable. Avoid the tourist-facing cafes near the airport - overpriced and mediocre.
From Gilgit

Where to
Go Next

2h north

Hunza Valley

Rakaposhi, Attabad Lake, Karimabad, apricot blossoms. GB's most famous valley and deservedly so. Shared jeep from Gilgit bus stand: Rs. 400-700 (confirm locally).

4-5h east

Skardu

Gateway to K2 and the Karakoram high valleys. Shigar, Deosai, Sheosar Lake. There are no Gilgit-Skardu flights - drive ~4-5h via Jaglot on the road along the Indus (allow 1-2h extra for 2026 flood repairs).

~2h south + jeep & trek

Fairy Meadows

Nanga Parbat base camp access from Raikot Bridge, ~76-80km (about 2h) south of Gilgit on the KKH, then a rough jeep ride and a 2-3h uphill walk. Overnight destination rather than a quick day trip.
