Near the City

Within
15km

Kargah Buddha — 10km. A 7th-century standing Buddha carved into a cliff face above the Kargah Nala gorge. Free. 30-minute visit. The most historically significant thing within day-trip distance of Gilgit, and the most overlooked by tourists. Essential.

Jutial viewpoint — The residential ridge above the main city, 3km from Saddar Bazaar. Walk or rickshaw up (Rs.100–150) for views over the entire Gilgit River confluence, the KKH corridor, and the surrounding mountains. Best at golden hour.

Gilgit River confluence — Where the Gilgit and Hunza rivers meet, about 5km northeast of town. You can see it from the road on the way to Hunza. The colour contrast between the sediment-laden Gilgit and the clearer Hunza is striking.

Gilgit Fort — Ruins of the old fort in the centre of town. Not much standing, but the site has good city views and historical significance as the pre-British administrative centre of the region.

Day Trips

30–60km
from Gilgit

Naltar Valley42km, 1.5hrs. Alpine lakes or skiing. Full day. Rs.4,000–6,000 jeep.
Bagrot Valley42km, 1.5hrs. Terraced fields, Diran Peak views. Half day or full day. Rs.3,500–5,000 jeep.
Nomal Valley34km north on KKH, then up valley. Quieter than Naltar, good for fishing. Half day.
Jaglot25km south on KKH. Where the Indus and Gilgit rivers meet — dramatic rocky confluence visible from the road. Stop en route to/from Islamabad.
Haramosh Valley40km east, then valley. Remote, trekking territory. 2+ days minimum to do justice.
Danyore20km south. Village known for wood carving and traditional crafts. Worth a stop if you're interested in local artisans.
