Overview

Gilgit in
Plain Terms

Gilgit is the administrative capital of Gilgit-Baltistan — a city of around 300,000 sitting at 1,500m where the Gilgit River meets the Hunza River. It's where all roads diverge: north goes to Hunza and China, east to Skardu, west to Ghizer and Chitral, south back down the KKH toward Islamabad.

Most tourists treat Gilgit as a transit point — land at the airport, get in a jeep, drive straight to Hunza. I understand why. But spending even one day here gives you something the polished tourist trail doesn't: a city that actually functions, with real markets, river views, and an ancient Buddhist carving 10 minutes from the city centre that most visitors completely miss.

Gilgit is also the warmest major town in GB — at 1,500m it can hit 38°C in July, which is brutal. The shoulder seasons (April–June and September–October) are when it's genuinely pleasant.

Flight reality: PIA and Airblue fly Islamabad–Gilgit daily. But the route is weather-dependent and delays are endemic. Plan a buffer day on both ends of any trip through Gilgit airport.

What to Do

Don't Just
Pass Through

Kargah Buddha — A 7th-century Buddhist rock carving 10km from the city centre. A standing figure (~3m tall) cut into a cliff face above the Kargah Nala gorge. It's one of the oldest monuments in GB and evidence of the Silk Road Buddhist culture that existed here long before Islam arrived. Free, 30-minute trip, almost no tourists. Full Kargah Buddha guide →

Naltar Valley — Pakistan's only proper ski resort, 42km from Gilgit. In winter (December–March) it has actual functioning ski runs. In summer (June–August) the valley has three stunning alpine lakes. A full-day trip from Gilgit. Naltar Valley guide →

Bagrot Valley — A green side-valley 42km from Gilgit with terraced wheat fields and traditional villages. Easily the most unexpectedly lush landscape near the city. Half-day jeep trip. Bagrot Valley guide →

Gilgit River Walk — The riverbank near Jutial has a decent walking track with mountain views. Early morning before the dust picks up.

Local Polo — Polo is played at the Gilgit Polo Ground during festival days. Ask at your guesthouse when the next match is. Free to watch, completely raw — no helmets, no padding, riders who seem to be made of a different substance than the rest of us.

Day trip from Gilgit: Kargah Buddha + Naltar in one day is very doable. Hire a jeep from Saddar Bazaar (Rs.6,000–10,000 for the full day). Most guesthouses can arrange this.
Food

Where to
Eat in Gilgit

Sardar RestaurantSaddar Bazaar. Cheap, reliable daal, rice, karahi. Under Rs.500/person. Where locals eat.
Hunzai Food HouseLocal GB cuisine — chapshuro (meat-filled flatbread), diram phitti, apricot soup. Rs.600–900.
PTDC Motel restaurantSit-down service, cleaner environment, slightly pricier (Rs.800–1,500). Good for groups.
Ittehad BakeryFresh naan and local bread, open from 6am. Rs.30–50 per naan.
River troutSeveral restaurants near Kargah serve fresh Gilgit River trout. Ask for it grilled. Worth it.
Chai dhabbasEverywhere. Milky pink Kashmiri chai or regular doodh pati. Rs.30–50 a cup.
Avoid: The "tourist restaurants" near the airport markup prices 3x. The food is identical to Saddar Bazaar at a third of the cost.
Getting There

Reaching
Gilgit

By air: PIA and Airblue fly Islamabad–Gilgit (55 minutes). Roughly Rs.8,000–15,000 one-way. The route uses ATR-72 turboprops — weather-dependent, with the Karakoram range requiring specific conditions. Budget a spare day for delays. Book the first flight of the day — cancellations cascade as the day goes on.

By road via KKH: Islamabad to Gilgit is 14–16 hours non-stop. Chilas (halfway) is a common overnight stop. The KKH is one of the world's great drives — along the Indus River, through the Karakoram — but it's long. Deluxe coaches (Skyways, Mashkarbrum) leave Rawalpindi's Pirwadhai bus stand daily from 6–8am.

From Skardu: 6–7 hours by road via Skardu–Gilgit highway (paved, good condition). Regular shared Coasters for Rs.600–800/person, jeep hire Rs.8,000–12,000.

ATM strategy: Withdraw cash in Gilgit before proceeding to Hunza or Skardu. ATMs exist further north but run out frequently. HBL and UBL on Saddar Bazaar are most reliable.
