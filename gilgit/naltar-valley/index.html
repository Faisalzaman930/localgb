Naltar Valley
Ski & Lakes

The Valley

Two Seasons,
Two Identities

Naltar does something rare in GB — it's genuinely worth visiting in both winter and summer, just for completely different reasons. The valley sits at 2,900m with pine forests, three distinct alpine lakes, and a ski slope that's operated by the Pakistan Air Force since 1983.

I took a group there in January once — the Blue Lake was half-frozen, the ski slope had maybe 20 people on it, and the silence broken only by the chairlift was unlike anything I've experienced in more famous places. It's surreal in the best way. You're standing at a functioning ski resort in Pakistan's mountains and there are no queues, no crowds, no overpriced coffee.

In summer, the three lakes — Blue, Red, and Golden — are among the most vivid alpine lakes in the region. Different mineral compositions give each one a distinct colour. The Blue Lake is the most famous and genuinely deserves the reputation.

Getting there: The road to Naltar goes via Nomal village (34km from Gilgit on the KKH), then a steep, mostly unpaved 8km track up to the valley. A 4WD jeep is essential — regular cars won't make it. Jeep hire from Gilgit bazaar: Rs.4,000–6,000 return.
Skiing

Pakistan's Only
Ski Resort

OperatorPakistan Air Force (PAF) — open to public during season
SeasonDecember – March (snow-dependent)
Slopes4 runs, beginner to intermediate, maximum 600m vertical
ChairliftOne T-bar lift, Rs.500–800/day
Equipment rentalAvailable on site — old but functional. Boots, skis, poles: Rs.800–1,200/day
AccommodationPAF Naltar Rest House (book in advance via PAF Gilgit), basic local guesthouses in Naltar village
Snow qualityDry powder December–January, heavier snow February. Best conditions: January
International skiers: This isn't Chamonix. The grooming is basic, the facilities are minimal, and the rental gear is dated. But the setting — pine forest, Karakoram views, genuine mountain atmosphere — is extraordinary. Go for the experience, not the infrastructure.
The Lakes

Blue, Red
& Golden

Blue Lake (Naltar Lake) — The most visited and most photographed. Sits at 3,050m, deep turquoise-blue from glacial minerals. The colour is genuinely that vivid — I've had guests accuse me of editing photos until they stood next to it. Trek from the ski resort: 2km, 45 minutes.

Red Lake — Higher than Blue Lake (3,600m), smaller, and a distinctly reddish-brown colour from iron oxide in the surrounding rock. Less visited. 2-hour trek from Blue Lake — steep but worth it for the contrast.

Golden Lake — The furthest and highest (3,700m). Smaller still, with a golden-amber hue in afternoon light. Requires a full day from the valley floor. Barely visited.

Best month for lakesJuly–August (stable weather, full snow melt)
Blue Lake day tripEasily doable from Gilgit in one day (leave by 7am, back by 6pm)
All three lakesRequires overnight in Naltar — stay at guesthouse in Naltar village (Rs.1,500–2,500/night)
Trek difficultyBlue Lake: easy. Red Lake: moderate. Golden Lake: strenuous.
