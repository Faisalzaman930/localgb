What to See

A Lake, a Dam
and a Buddha

Satpara is Skardu's drinking water reservoir, sitting just 9km south of the city - about a 15-minute drive. Most people come for the calm green water and the easy half-day it makes, but there is more here than a photo stop. The Satpara Dam supplies drinking water and small-scale hydropower to Skardu city, and the lake is stocked with brown and rainbow trout, so anglers come up with a permit for the day.

The quiet highlight is the Satpara Buddha, a Buddhist rock carving, probably from the 8th century, in Manthal village on the road up from Skardu - a reminder that this valley sat on old trans-Himalayan routes long before it was a reservoir. It is easy to miss, so ask locals to point you to it. Because the lake pairs so easily with the cold desert at Katpana, across town near the airport, many visitors fold both into a single unhurried morning.

FAQ

Common
Questions

How far is Satpara Lake from Skardu?

It is 9km south of Skardu, roughly a 15-minute drive.

Can you fish at Satpara Lake?

Yes. The lake holds brown and rainbow trout. You need a daily permit from the Fisheries Department office in Skardu (around Rs.500-1,000 in past seasons - confirm the current fee).

What is the Satpara Buddha?

A Buddhist rock carving, probably from the 8th century, in Manthal village on the road up from Skardu. It is easy to miss, so ask locals for directions.

Is there an entry fee?

There is a small vehicle entry charge, around Rs.100 - confirm on arrival.

How long do I need at the lake?

It works well as a half-day trip, and you can easily combine it with the Katpana Desert, across town near the airport, in a single morning.
