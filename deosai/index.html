National Park

Deosai Plains

An ocean of wildflowers at 4,114m. Asia's second-highest plateau, home to the Himalayan brown bear - and one of the most disorienting landscapes in Pakistan.

📍 Astore / Skardu District
⛰ 4,114m avg. elevation
🐻 Himalayan brown bear
🌿 Best: late Jun - Sep
Home / Skardu / Deosai Plains
By Faisal Zaman·Local from Gilgit-Baltistan·Updated June 2026
Overview

The Land
of Giants

Deosai - meaning "land of giants" in Shina - is a plateau that covers 3,000 km² at an average altitude of 4,114m, making it the second-highest plateau in Asia after Tibet. From June to September it is entirely carpeted in wildflowers: primroses, gentians, buttercups, and dozens of species found nowhere else.

The plateau was declared a National Park in 1993 specifically to protect the last remaining population of Himalayan brown bear. That population has grown from around 19 bears in the early 1990s to over 50 today. Sightings are common in July and August when bears are foraging on the open grassland.

Sheosar Lake, a cobalt glacial lake at the plateau's western edge, is the most visited spot. From its northern shore you can see the lake, the plateau, and the peaks of the Greater Karakoram in a single frame.

Logistics note: Deosai is only accessible by 4WD jeep. The road from Skardu is unsealed and crosses several stream fords. Do not attempt it in a regular car. River crossings can be impassable after heavy rain.
Wildlife

What Lives
on the Plateau

Deosai has one of the densest concentrations of wildlife of any accessible plateau in South Asia.

Himalayan Brown Bear
Ursus arctos isabellinus

The star resident. Best sightings in July-August when bears forage on the open plateau. Do not approach or leave food accessible at campsites. Population: ~50.

Snow Leopard
Panthera uncia

Present on the plateau margins and rocky slopes. Sightings are rare and usually at dusk or dawn. Camera traps show regular activity near the plateau's eastern edge.

Marco Polo Sheep
Ovis ammon polii

Large-horned sheep seen in herds on the higher plateau sections. Named after the Venetian traveller who described them in the 13th century.

Golden Eagle
Aquila chrysaetos

Commonly seen riding thermals above the plateau. Wingspan up to 2.2m. Often visible from Sheosar Lake in the morning.

Tibetan Wolf
Canis lupus chanco

Present but elusive. Tracks visible near the plateau's streams in early morning. Rarely seen directly but often heard at night near campsites.

Ladakh Urial
Ovis vignei vignei

Wild sheep found on the drier rocky sections. Smaller than Marco Polo sheep. Often seen in groups of 10-20 near the Astore entry point.

Getting There

Access &
Logistics

From SkarduThe main access route. Roughly 3-4h by 4WD jeep via Sadpara Lake and the Burji La pass to reach the plateau (longer to push on to Sheosar Lake, which sits deep inside). Hire a jeep in Skardu: Rs. 8,000-15,000 per day (negotiable). Most visitors do a day trip, but camping is strongly recommended.
From AstoreAlternative access via Chilum Chowk. 3-4h from Astore town. Rougher track but less traffic. Gives access to the eastern plateau sections.
Entry feeA park entry fee is collected at the checkpoint and is payable at the gate - indicative only and worth confirming before you travel, as rates change often. Pakistani nationals pay a modest fee (commonly cited around Rs. 500); foreigners pay considerably more (figures in the range of roughly PKR 3,000 / USD 20-40 have been reported). Keep your receipt - it may be checked at Sheosar.
Jeep hireHire in Skardu city. Ask your guesthouse for recommendations. Agree the route and return time before departure. Include Sheosar Lake and Bara Pani in your itinerary.
FuelNo fuel available on the plateau. Ensure your jeep starts with a full tank from Skardu.
Practical Info

What You
Need to Know

Best monthsLate June - September, with late June and July often the most rewarding for fresh wildflowers. The park usually opens around mid-to-late June once the snow clears, and early June can still have snow patches and mud on the tracks. October gets cold very fast - nights below -10°C possible. Exact opening shifts year to year with snowfall, so confirm before you travel.
CampingAllowed. Designated camping areas at Sheosar Lake and Bara Pani. No facilities. Bring everything. Campfire wood is not available - bring a gas stove.
Altitude4,114m average. Acclimatise in Skardu (2,228m) for at least two days before a full Deosai day, especially if flying in from Islamabad.
WeatherConditions change fast. Sunny mornings can become hail within 2 hours. Bring full waterproofs even on clear days. Temperature range: 5°C-18°C in summer.
No connectivityNo mobile signal on the plateau. No electricity. Download maps offline before you go (Maps.me has good coverage).
FoodA few chai stalls operate near Sheosar Lake in July-August. Don't rely on them. Pack meals for the day and overnight if camping.
Key Spots

Places on
the Plateau

Lake · Western plateau

Sheosar Lake

A glacially-fed lake at around 4,150m (sources give 4,142-4,250m). The water is cobalt blue against the surrounding green plateau and snow peaks. Best light is early morning. The lake freezes over October-June and can have ice sheets even in late June.

Meadow · Central plateau

Bara Pani

A large open meadow in the plateau's centre - the best area for brown bear sightings. Scan the slopes in early morning and late afternoon. The camping here is exceptional: no wind shelter, full sky exposure, no light pollution.

Pass · Entry point from Skardu

Burji La

The pass connecting Skardu's Sadpara Valley to the Deosai plateau. The moment you cross and the plateau opens up is genuinely startling - you go from canyon to flat infinity in about 100 metres.
