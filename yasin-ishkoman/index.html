Yasin & Ishkoman Valleys

Two side valleys branching off the main Ghizer corridor

Overview

The Valleys
No One Goes To

Yasin and Ishkoman are two side valleys branching north from the main Ghizer corridor — collectively the least-visited inhabited valleys in all of Gilgit-Baltistan. They have no famous lakes, no polo festivals, no high-altitude passes accessible to casual tourists, and no accommodation infrastructure beyond family homestays. They also have some of the most genuine, unhurried mountain life you will find anywhere in Pakistan.

Both valleys are inhabited by communities that have had minimal contact with outside tourism. The Kho people of Ishkoman and the mixed Kho/Burusho/Wakhi communities of Yasin speak different languages, have distinct musical traditions, and live at the far edge of what roads connect. The hospitality is legendary even by GB standards.

These valleys are not for the casual visitor. They require time (minimum 2 days each to see anything meaningful), tolerance for very basic accommodation, 4WD transport, and an NOC for upper valley access near the Afghan border. But for travellers who have done Hunza and want to find something that feels genuinely undiscovered — this is it.

Honest assessment: If you've never been to GB before, do Hunza, Skardu, and Fairy Meadows first. Yasin and Ishkoman are for return visitors or those specifically seeking remoteness over scenery rankings. The valleys are beautiful but the experience is primarily cultural and experiential, not visual spectacle.
The Two Valleys

Yasin vs Ishkoman:
What to Expect

Ghizer District · 2,200m · 5h from Gilgit
Yasin Valley

Yasin is the more accessible and slightly more visited of the two. The valley runs north from the Ghizer main road at a junction before Gupis, climbing through terraced farmland to Yasin town at 2,200m, then continuing into a dramatic river gorge toward the Darkot Pass area.

What's here

The valley has traditional stone villages perched above the river, apricot and mulberry orchards in season (July–August), and a local bazaar in Yasin town that functions as a trading hub for surrounding communities. The upper valley approach to Darkot Pass (5,704m) — a historic Silk Road crossing to Chitral — is a serious high-altitude trek for experienced parties only.

Sandhi Gol

A side valley off Yasin, rarely visited even by local trekkers, with alpine meadows at 3,500m+. No formal trail but locals will guide you. 1-2 day walk from the road. The kind of place where you will not see another footprint.

Culture

The Kho and Burusho communities of Yasin have preserved musical traditions including the rubab (short-necked lute) and distinctive circle dances. Wednesday is the weekly market day in Yasin town — the best time to arrive.

Ghizer District · 2,400m · 6–7h from Gilgit
Ishkoman Valley

Ishkoman branches west from Chatorkhand on the Ghizer road, running toward the Afghan border through one of the most isolated inhabited corridors in GB. The Kho people here are culturally distinct from the Wakhi of Gojal and the Shina-speakers of Astore — a community that maintained independence from external rule until the 19th century.

What's here

Ishkoman town sits at 2,400m, ringed by peaks above 6,000m. The Ishkoman Glacier at the valley's head is reachable by a 2-day trek from town. Upper Ishkoman near the Afghan border requires an NOC — the road beyond a certain checkpoint is restricted. The Chillinji Pass area (above Ishkoman) connects to the Chapursan Valley in upper Gojal for experienced trekkers.

What it feels like

Of all the valleys covered on this site, Ishkoman is the one where local children will stare openly at a tourist because they have genuinely never seen one. The hospitality is extreme — declining an invitation to stay the night is considered rude. If you have time for only one completely off-grid valley in GB, Ishkoman is the answer.

Highlights

What to See
& Do

Yasin · Wednesday · farming community
Yasin Weekly Bazaar
Every Wednesday, farmers and traders from villages throughout the Yasin Valley converge on Yasin town. Livestock, local honey, dried mulberries and apricots, hand-spun wool, medicinal herbs. Almost no outside visitors. This is the most authentic market in Ghizer District — more agricultural than touristic, which is exactly its value. Arrive Tuesday evening and stay in a local guesthouse.
Yasin · 2-day walk · 3,500m meadow
Sandhi Gol Alpine Meadow
A high alpine side-valley above Yasin with meadows at 3,500m that are completely unmarked on any tourist map and rarely visited even by local hikers. The walk from the road takes 5–6h and requires a local guide (ask in Yasin town — PKR 1,500–2,500/day). Camp overnight. The meadow has wildflowers in July and is surrounded by glaciated peaks above 5,000m. No fixed route, no infrastructure.
Ishkoman · 2-day trek · guide required
Ishkoman Glacier
The glacier at the head of Ishkoman Valley is reached by a 2-day walk from Ishkoman town. The approach passes through small hamlets where visitors are extremely rare. The glacier itself is a classic hanging glacier with an impressive terminal face. No technical equipment needed for the approach but the terrain is rough and unmarked. Guide from Ishkoman town: PKR 2,000–3,000/day.
Extreme trek · 5,704m · Silk Road history
Darkot Pass Approach
The Darkot Pass (5,704m) above Yasin was historically the main Silk Road crossing between Yasin and Chitral — used for centuries when Khunjerab Pass didn't exist as a formal route. The pass is now rarely crossed. The approach trek from Yasin is 4–5 days to the pass and back, through dramatic high-altitude terrain. NOC required, guide and full camping gear essential. For serious trekkers only.
Ishkoman · traditional Kho community
Kho Village Homestays
The Kho communities in the upper Ishkoman villages — Barset, Pakora, Chatibai — have received almost no outside visitors. Arriving in one of these villages and asking for accommodation will result in an overnight stay in a family home with home-cooked food, local music, and conversations that require a local translator unless you speak Khowar. These are not organised homestays — they are genuine hospitality. Bring a small gift (sugar, tea, biscuits).
Both valleys · June–August
Apricot & Mulberry Season
Both Yasin and Ishkoman valleys produce apricots and mulberries that are, arguably, better than those in Hunza — because they're not being farmed for the tourist market. The fruit is larger, less uniform, and eaten fresh from the tree or dried on flat rooftops in the sun. Visit in July for fresh fruit. The dried apricots from Yasin (sold at the Wednesday market) are among the finest in GB.
Getting There

Access &
Permits

NOC for upper valleys: The upper reaches of both Yasin and Ishkoman (beyond certain checkposts, particularly near the Afghan border areas) require an NOC from the Deputy Commissioner's office in Gahkuch (Ghizer HQ). The lower and mid valleys — Yasin town, lower Ishkoman — are generally accessible with just CNIC/Passport. Ask at the first checkpoint about current restrictions.
Gilgit to YasinShared jeep via Gahkuch: PKR 600–1,000 (4–5h). Private jeep: PKR 9,000–15,000. The Yasin turnoff is before Gupis on the Ghizer road.
Gilgit to IshkomanShared jeep to Chatorkhand: PKR 700–1,200 (5–6h). Then local jeep to Ishkoman town: PKR 400–700. Private jeep Gilgit to Ishkoman: PKR 12,000–18,000.
Within valleysLocal jeeps (often the only shared transport for the day) connect villages. Expect to wait. Alternatively, walk — the valley floors are flat and the distances between villages are manageable on foot.
NOC processApply at DC office, Gahkuch (Ghizer district HQ). Takes 1–2 days. Bring CNIC (Pakistani nationals) or passport + visa (foreign visitors). State your itinerary clearly. The NOC is generally issued without difficulty for tourist purposes.
Mobile signalJazz has signal in Yasin town and lower Ishkoman. Signal drops entirely in upper valleys. Download offline maps (Maps.me or OsmAnd) before leaving Gilgit.
Practical Info

What to Expect
on the Ground

Accommodation

There is no formal hotel sector in either valley. Accommodation means family homestays (PKR 500–1,500 per person including meals) or camping. In Yasin town there are 2–3 small guesthouses (PKR 1,200–2,500). Announce your arrival at the local checkpost — they typically know which families take guests. In upper village areas, the tradition of offering shelter to travellers remains strong; you will not sleep outside.

Food

Dal, chapati, rice, and eggs at guesthouses. In family homes: whatever the family is cooking — often more interesting. Local honey, fresh fruit in season, and hand-churned butter are genuine highlights. Bring supplementary snacks (dry fruit, nuts, biscuits) for treks where you won't pass a settlement.

When to Go

June and September are ideal — roads open, crowds non-existent (they were already non-existent), and weather stable. July–August brings occasional rain in the lower Ghizer valleys. Avoid November–April when upper roads become impassable.

Who Should Come

Travellers who want: genuine remoteness, cultural contact with communities untouched by tourism, and the particular satisfaction of being somewhere without a tourist infrastructure. Not suitable for those who need reliable accommodation, predictable schedules, or mobile signal. Suitable for those who've already done the main GB circuit and want to go further.
