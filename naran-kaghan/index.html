Valley Guide

Naran &
Kaghan Valley

A glacial valley in KPK's Mansehra District with some of Pakistan's most accessible alpine scenery. Babusar Pass links it directly to GB and the Karakoram Highway.

📍 Mansehra, KPK
⛰ 2,409m Naran town
🏔 4,173m Babusar Pass
🌿 Best: Jun – Sep

Overview

Pakistan's Most
Visited Valley

Naran Kaghan is the most visited alpine destination in Pakistan, drawing over 1.5 million domestic tourists each summer. That number matters: in July and August, Naran town can feel overwhelmed. But the valley extends far beyond the tourist strip, and the surrounding lakes and passes are genuinely exceptional.

The valley follows the Kunhar River north from Balakot to Babusar Top. Lake Saiful Muluk at 3,224m is the jewel — a glacial lake ringed by snow peaks, accessible by jeep. Further north, Lulusar Lake and Babusar Pass (4,173m) mark the entry into Kaghan's wilder upper reaches and connect to Chilas on the KKH.

For travellers doing Gilgit-Baltistan, the Naran–Babusar–Chilas route is one of the great alternative approaches to GB: a full day of mountain driving that bypasses the Islamabad–Chilas KKH stretch and passes through dramatically different terrain.

Crowd management: Avoid Naran town the last two weeks of July and all of August if you can. Visit in June (just after snow clears) or September (cool, clear, empty). The scenery is identical; the experience is completely different.
Key Attractions

What to See
in the Valley

Lake · 3,224m · 10km from Naran

Lake Saiful Muluk

Pakistan's most famous lake. Glacial, turquoise-green, ringed by snow peaks including Malika Parbat (5,291m). Jeep from Naran: Rs. 2,000–3,000 return. Morning is calmer and clearer. The Urdu folk legend of a prince falling in love with a fairy princess here is why it's culturally iconic.

Pass · 4,173m · Connects to KKH

Babusar Pass

The high pass connecting Kaghan Valley to Chilas on the KKH. Open June to late October (snow permitting). The drive across is one of the most scenic in Pakistan. From the top you can see both the Himalayas and Karakoram. Jeep only — no cars.

Lake · 3,440m · 20km from Naran

Lulusar Lake

Larger and quieter than Saiful Muluk. Sits in an open plateau below the Babusar Pass road. Reflects the surrounding peaks perfectly on calm mornings. Often visited en route to Babusar. Few tourists make the extra effort to stop — which means it's usually empty.

Trek · 2 days · 4,245m

Ansoo Lake

A remote lake shaped like a teardrop, at 4,245m above Saiful Muluk. Two-day trek with camping. The trail is steep and requires a guide (available in Naran: Rs. 2,500–4,000/day). One of the more demanding but rewarding treks in the Kaghan Valley.

River · Lower valley

Kunhar River & Trout

The Kunhar is one of Pakistan's best trout rivers. Fishing permits available in Naran (Rs. 1,000–2,000/day). Several guesthouses serve fresh trout — it's genuinely good. The riverside camping sites below Naran are calmer alternatives to the town's guesthouses.

Town · Lower valley gateway

Balakot & Shogran

Balakot is the entry point to the valley, 2h from Abbottabad. Shogran, above Balakot, is a forested ridge at 2,362m with views of Musa Ka Musalla (4,080m). Quieter and cooler than Naran. Good base for the lower valley exploration.

Getting There

How to
Reach Naran

From Islamabad4–5h by road via Abbottabad and Balakot. NATCO and private coaches from Rawalpindi's Pir Wadhai bus terminal (Rs. 600–1,200). Private car: Rs. 8,000–14,000. Road is paved throughout.
From Lahore7–8h via the Motorway and Abbottabad. NATCO departs Lahore bus terminal daily in peak season (Rs. 1,000–1,800).
From GB (via Babusar)From Chilas on the KKH, cross Babusar Pass to Naran (3–4h, June–October only). This is the scenic route for GB-to-Naran travellers. Jeep hire from Chilas: Rs. 8,000–12,000.
Within the valleyLocal jeeps connect Naran to Saiful Muluk (Rs. 2,000–3,000 return), Lulusar (Rs. 3,000–4,000), and Babusar (Rs. 4,000–6,000). Share with other travellers to reduce cost.
Staying

Practical
Notes

AccommodationNaran has 50+ guesthouses at all price points. Budget: Rs. 1,500–3,000. Mid-range: Rs. 4,000–8,000. Book 2 weeks ahead for July–August. Hotels are severely overpriced at walk-in in peak season — book online.
Best monthsJune & September strongly recommended over July–August. Babusar is accessible June–October. Saiful Muluk can have snow even in June — call ahead.
AltitudeNaran is at 2,409m. No acclimatisation needed for most visitors. Babusar Pass at 4,173m can cause mild altitude sickness — don't rush the ascent.
ConnectivityJazz and Telenor have reasonable signal in Naran town and Saiful Muluk. Signal drops above Lulusar. Download offline maps.
Note on crowdsNaran's main road becomes gridlocked in peak August. What should be a 2h valley drive can take 6h. This is not an exaggeration. Go in June or September.
The Connection

Naran to
Gilgit-Baltistan

The Babusar Pass route is how many experienced Pakistan travellers connect Naran Kaghan to the Karakoram Highway and onward to Gilgit and Hunza. It adds genuine mountain drama to a journey that would otherwise spend 18 hours on the KKH lowlands.

Typical itinerary: Islamabad → Naran (2 nights) → Babusar Pass → Chilas → Fairy Meadows (2 nights) → Gilgit → Hunza. This is approximately 10 days and covers the complete mountain corridor from KPK into the Karakoram.

The Babusar Pass road is open roughly June 15 – October 15, depending on snowfall. Check with the Naran police checkpoint (they post updates) before attempting the crossing. It closes without warning after early snowfall.
