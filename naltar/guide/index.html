Naltar Valley Guide

Three alpine lakes of different colours, Pakistan's only functioning ski resort, and dense spruce forest — 65km from Gilgit and almost always empty.

Overview

Naltar in
Plain Terms

Naltar is a side valley 65km north of Gilgit city, branching east off the Karakoram Highway near Nomal village. Despite being just two hours from Gilgit — and within striking distance of millions of Hunza-bound tourists who pass the turnoff every season — it remains conspicuously undervisited. Most travellers bypass the Nomal junction entirely.

The valley is famous for three things: its three glacially-formed alpine lakes that each hold a different colour (deep blue, emerald green, and a purple-grey tinted upper lake); the Pakistan Air Force ski resort, which is the country's only functioning ski area with mechanical lifts; and the extraordinary spruce and pine forest that covers the middle elevations — dense enough to feel alpine rather than high-desert, which is exceptional in northern Pakistan.

The upper valley extends to passes linking with Daintar and Ishkoman valleys, making Naltar a serious multi-day trekking corridor for those who explore beyond the lakes. Snow leopard, Himalayan ibex, brown bear, and golden eagle are all confirmed residents of the upper valley.

Access reality: There is no public transport beyond Nomal village. A jeep is essential — either hired from Nomal (Rs.3,000–5,000 one way) or from Gilgit (Rs.7,000–10,000 return with driver waiting). Do not attempt the jeep track in a sedan.

Getting There

How to
Reach Naltar

Gilgit to Nomal30km north on KKH. Shared Suzuki Rs.100, 45 minutes. Nomal is the junction — turn right off the KKH.
Nomal to Naltar35km jeep track, 1.5h. Rough road — passable in standard jeep, not for sedans. Jeep hire from Nomal Rs.3,000–5,000 one way (negotiate for return or wait).
Direct from GilgitPrivate jeep hire Rs.7,000–10,000 return including driver waiting. Best option for day trips. Multiple agencies in Gilgit city can arrange.
By airPIA flies Islamabad→Gilgit (45min, Rs.5,000–12,000, frequent cancellations). Naltar has a PAF airstrip but it is closed to civilians. Gilgit Airport is the nearest commercial option.
From HunzaDrive or take transport south on KKH to Nomal junction. No direct route exists that bypasses Gilgit. Allow 1.5–2h from Karimabad to Nomal.
Road condition: The Naltar jeep track is rough but well-used by locals. Snow at higher sections typically closes the upper road October through April. The lower portions may remain accessible. Confirm current status with Nomal locals before committing.
What's Up There

Naltar's Key
Attractions

Three-Colour LakesBlue Lake (3,050m), Green Lake (3,150m), Upper Lake (3,200m). 3km walk from village, 2–3h round trip. Entry Rs.300.
PAF Ski ResortDecember–February. Pakistan's only ski resort with chairlift. Civilian access with advance booking. Rs.1,500–2,500/day.
Wildlife watchingSnow leopard pugmarks near glacier. Ibex on rocky slopes above treeline. Brown bear in upper valley. Early morning optimal.
Forest walksSpruce and pine forest between village and lakes — excellent birdwatching. Koklass pheasant, Himalayan monal, lammergeyer.
Naltar Pass trekMulti-day onward to Daintar Valley (connects to Ishkoman). Guide essential. June–September only.

Wildlife of
Upper Naltar

The combination of dense conifer forest and alpine meadows above 3,000m creates one of the best wildlife habitats in the Gilgit division. The forest floor at 2,800–3,100m is among the most productive birding terrain in northern Pakistan.

Snow leopardPugmarks confirmed near glaciers above 3,500m. Almost never seen by day visitors but camera trap records are consistent.
Himalayan ibexRocky slopes above treeline. Groups of 10–30 animals. Visible from the trail near Upper Lake with binoculars.
Brown bearUpper valley above 3,200m. Not aggressive unless surprised. Locals advise making noise when off-trail.
Golden eaglePairs nest on cliff faces above the lakes. Regularly seen soaring on thermals by mid-morning.
Koklass pheasantForest understorey near Naltar village. Heard more often than seen — distinctive rattling call at dawn.
Practical Planning

Day Trip vs
Overnight

Naltar works as a day trip from Gilgit — depart by 7am, walk the lakes, return by 5pm. But the valley rewards an overnight stay: early mornings before other visitors arrive give completely different photography conditions, and the forest walk in low light is excellent for birds.

Day trip itineraryDepart Gilgit 7am → Nomal 7:45am → Naltar village 9:15am → Lakes walk 9:30am–1pm → Lunch at PAF Rest House → Return Gilgit 4pm
Overnight itineraryDay 1: Arrive, settle, evening forest walk. Day 2: Lakes at 7am (before other visitors), photography, explore upper meadows. Return afternoon.
Ski trip (winter)Book PAF Rest House in advance. Drive up day before (road may need clearance). Ski 2–3 days, return Gilgit.
Multi-day trekNaltar village → Naltar Pass → Daintar Valley. 3–4 days. Guide required. June–September only.
