## 2026 unrest, shutdowns and politics
- **[NEW] Mar 2026 unrest:** protests after Khamenei was killed in US-Israeli strikes on Iran turned violent in Gilgit and Skardu on **1 Mar 2026**. About 13–14 people were killed in GB (Pamir Times figures are internally inconsistent). Curfews were imposed in Gilgit and Skardu, the army was deployed, and GB-wide mobile service was suspended indefinitely, then partly restored from 2 Mar. A Green Tourism guest house in Skardu was burned. The News (13 Mar) reports an "11-day curfew". — High (events) / Med (death toll and curfew length) — https://pamirtimes.net/2026/03/02/gilgit-baltistan-unrest-13-dead-curfew-imposed-army-deployed-after-violent-protests-over-assassination-of-irans-supreme-leader/ (2 Mar 2026); https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/3/2/pakistan-calls-troops-orders-3-day-curfew-as-24-killed-in-pro-iran-rallies (2 Mar 2026)
- **[NEW] GB Assembly elections were held 7 Jun 2026.** PPP is the largest party (15/33) and governs in coalition with PML-N. CM **Amjad Hussain Azar** was elected 22 Jun and sworn in 6 Jul 2026. No poll-day curfew or shutdown was reported, but PPP supporters **blocked the KKH at Chilas ~20 Jun** over the GBA-16 result. — Med-High — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Gilgit_Baltistan_Assembly_election
- **[NEW] Jul 2026 monsoon and GLOF floods:** from ~22 Jul, roads were cut, including Jaglot–Skardu, the Baltistan Highway (a bridge washed away), Gilgit–Astore, part of the Deosai road, Haramosh, Naltar, Hispar and Hushe. Hundreds of tourists were stranded in Skardu and Astore, internet was disrupted (fibre damage), and there were fuel and food shortages. The KKH and Gilgit–Shandur road had reopened by 25–26 Jul. — High — https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/2018405 (26 Jul 2026); https://tribune.com.pk/story/2620146/monsoon-fury-isolates-baltistan-region (25 Jul 2026)

- No reports found of curfews or shutdowns in GB for Aug–mid Oct 2026. — Med (absence of evidence)

## Visas
- **[CHANGED / NEW] The free "Visa Prior to Arrival" (VPA) for 126 nationalities was suspended from 1 Jan 2026** without a formal announcement, and no reinstatement had been reported as of Apr 2026 or in my searches. Travellers now pay for a standard e-visa. Wego says visa-on-arrival is also suspended; SAIGA says the ETA/VOA route still exists for ~50 nationalities. Recommend "apply for an e-visa in advance". — Med-High — https://discover.passportindex.org/policy-and-regulations/pakistan-suspends-free-visa-prior-to-arrival-for-126-nationalities/ (22 Jan 2026); https://blog.wego.com/pakistan-evisa/ (30 Apr 2026); https://www.saigatours.com/article/How-to-get-a-pakistan-e-visa-2026-update
- **[NEW] Single-entry 3-month tourist e-visa fees:** **US/UK/Canada ~USD 60; EU ~USD 35**; others ~USD 20–75. Free (except work visas) for Afghanistan, China, Japan, Iceland, North Korea, Slovakia, Malaysia, UAE and Saudi Arabia. Verify on the official fee calculator. — Med — Wego (30 Apr 2026); Passport Index (22 Jan 2026); calculator: visa.nadra.gov.pk/fee-structure
- **[NEW] Processing:** officially ~7–10 working days; reports run up to 20 working days. Apply 4–6 weeks ahead. — Med — Wego; SAIGA (2026)
- **[NEW] Official portal:** **visa.nadra.gov.pk** (Pakistan Online Visa System). A hotel booking is sufficient; a letter of invitation (LOI) is needed only when staying with a private host. — High (portal) / Med (LOI) — SAIGA; Wego (2026)

## Foreigner registration, NOC and restricted areas
- **[CONFIRMED] NOC abolished for GB tourism (Mar 2019).** **[CHANGED / NEW nuance]:** FCDO (Oct 2026) still says all foreigners "must register with the local authorities" in GB. In practice this means passport entries at police checkposts (e.g. the Chapursan checkpoint), so carry several passport and visa photocopies. — High (FCDO) / Med (practice) — FCDO regional risks (14 Oct 2026); https://www.lostwithpurpose.com/chapursan-guide (2021)
- **[CHANGED / corrected] Buffer zones:** Apricot Tours (undated) lists a 10 mi LoC belt, a **30 mi Wakhan belt**, and a 10 mi China border belt, and says foreigners may not go cross-country within 30 mi of the KKH. FCDO uses "10 miles" for the LoC and Afghan border advisories. FACTS.md says "LoC 5mi", which conflicts with both. Use "10 mi LoC" and flag the rest as Low. — Low-Med — https://www.apricottours.pk/?p=37376 (undated); FCDO (Oct 2026)
- **[CONFIRMED] Restricted treks needing a permit:** Baltoro–Gondogoro–Hushe, Baltoro–Masherbrum Pass, Hushe valley routes, Chapursan (Satura and upper valley), and Shimshal–Chapchingal Pass / Shimshal–Braldu–Snow Lake. Arrange through a licensed Pakistani operator. — Med — Apricot Tours (undated)
- **[NEW] Chapursan:** the main valley needs only checkpoint registration. Going **beyond Baba Ghundi** needs a DC Gilgit/Hunza NOC (2019–21 info). — Low-Med — Lost With Purpose (2021)
- **[NEW] CKNP Core Zone** (Baltoro, Biafo, Hispar, Snow Lake, Hushe–Masherbrum approach) needs a CKNP trekking permit. No solo trekking. Groups of 3+ need a registered guide, Gondogoro La needs an ACP-certified guide, and the porter limit is 25 kg. Upper Baltoro is closed in May; core glaciers are closed to high trekking Oct–Apr. — Low-Med (cknp.org is not clearly an official GB govt site, and its royalty figures contradict official ones) — https://www.cknp.org/visiting-cknp-permits-season-logistics/ (6 May 2026)
- **[NEW] Gopleiades says** a beyond-Sost or Khunjerab "special permit" costs USD 10–30. **Not corroborated, do not publish.** Normal Khunjerab day trips need only the park fee. — Low — https://gopleiades.com/?p=13878 (Aug 2025)

## Mountaineering royalties and trekking permits
- **[CHANGED / resolved] Outcome of the 2025 fee dispute:** in Sep 2024 GB planned individual permits at ~3x the per-head cost (K2 $5,000 pp), a rise of about 300%. PATO won a GB Chief Court stay in Mar 2025, and permits stalled with 200+ foreigners waiting. On **13 May 2025** the GB cabinet cut fees: **K2 summer USD 3,500 per person (individual, not collective)**, cuts of up to 50% for other peaks over 7,000 m, and reduced trekking fees. Pakistanis pay PKR 50,000 (summer) / 30,000 (winter) for K2. — High — https://www.zameen.com/news/gb-revises-fee-structure-for-mountain-climbing.html (28 May 2025); https://www.thenews.com.pk/print/1315089-gb-slashes-mountaineering-trekking-fees-to-promote-tourism (25 May 2025); https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/1900280 (26 Mar 2025)
- **[CHANGED] 2026 royalties are unchanged from 2025 and per person:** K2 USD 3,500; the other four 8,000ers USD 2,500 (summer); 7,000 m peaks ~USD 500 and higher 6,000ers ~USD 600 (K2 area). Some 6,000 m peaks near Nanga Parbat have no fee; some 5,700–6,400 m Central Karakoram peaks need only a trekking permit. Autumn and winter are cheaper. FACTS.md's "2025 collective" wording is wrong because the system is now individual. — High — https://explorersweb.com/despite-last-years-price-hikes-pakistan-remains-a-bargain-for-high-altitude-mountaineering/ (9 Feb 2026)
- **[UNRESOLVED] Trekking permit fee (restricted zone):** no 2025/26 figure has been published in the sources found. The pre-2025 schedule was USD 50 pp/month plus USD 150 environment fee; the cancelled 2025 hike averaged ~USD 500. Keep "~USD 50–200 pp + fees, confirm with operator". — Low — https://hunzaguidespakistan.com/tag/k2-climbing-permit-fee/ (2024 table); Dawn (26 Mar 2025)
- **[CHANGED] CKNP fee:** cknp.org gives "~PKR 2,000 per foreign trekker per trek, half for Pakistanis", says the trekking permit itself is free, and says it is paid in Skardu (2026). Hunza Guides (2024) gives USD 200 per expedition member. FACTS.md says "~$150". Sources disagree by roughly 25x, so publish as **range only: PKR 2,000 to USD 200**. — Low — cknp.org (6 May 2026); Hunza Guides (2024)
- **[NEW] Lead time:** FCDO says permissions take "up to 2 months". Operators quote 4–6 weeks. — Med-High — FCDO (Oct 2026); Hunza Guides

## SIM, connectivity and phones
- **[NEW] SIM purchase:** buy at a franchise or airport kiosk with passport, visa and biometric thumbprint. Max **2 SIMs per visit**, valid only while the visa is valid. — Med — https://esimcard.com/blog/sim-card/pakistan-sim-card/
- **[NEW] Phone (IMEI) registration:** PTA DRS lets foreigners register one personal phone **free for up to 120 days from arrival**. The phone is paired with a Pakistani SIM in your name, and you must reapply each visit. Without registration, a local SIM stops working in the phone; roaming or an eSIM needs no registration. — High — https://propakistani.pk/2026/06/18/how-overseas-pakistanis-can-register-their-mobile-phones-in-pakistan-for-120-days/amp/ (18 Jun 2026); PTA FAQ https://www.pta.gov.pk/assets/media/2025-07-23-temp-reg-FAQs.pdf (Jul 2025)
- **[NEW] Coverage:** **SCOM** (SCO) has the widest GB coverage. Jazz, Zong, Telenor and Ufone work in Gilgit, Hunza, Skardu and along the KKH but fade off main roads. Zong packages are not always available in GB. — Med — esimcard.com; https://nomad.watch/cities/PK/skardu/guide (Mar 2026, AI-generated, Low)

## Cash and ATMs
- **[NEW]** ATMs that accept foreign cards are best at **Dubai Islamic Bank and Faysal Bank**. HBL, Meezan, Alfalah, UBL, MCB, Allied and Standard Chartered also accept them at higher fees. Foreign cards are charged ~PKR 600–1,200 per withdrawal, with typical limits of PKR 50,000 per transaction. Decline the ATM's currency conversion (DCC). — Med — https://atmfeesaver.com/cash-currency-exchange-atms-in-pakistan/ (27 Feb 2026)
- **[NEW]** In GB, ATMs are limited in Hunza and foreign cards "don't always work". Skardu has HBL/NBP/Meezan ATMs. Carry enough PKR from Islamabad and expect a cash economy. — Med (Hunza) / Low (Skardu branch list) — gopleiades (Aug 2025); nomad.watch (Mar 2026)
