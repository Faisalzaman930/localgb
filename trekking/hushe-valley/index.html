Charakusa / Hushe Valley Trek 2026: Operators & Costs Compared

By Faisal Zaman·Local, Gilgit-Baltistan·Updated June 2026

The Hushe valley is the gateway to the Charakusa - a granite amphitheatre under K6, K7 and Laila Peak that climbers rate among the most beautiful on earth. Here is who runs treks here and what they charge in 2026.

I compare operators with a published price, with notes on what each includes. Trips range from valley walks to multi-week K6/K7 base-camp treks.

About the Charakusa / Hushe Valley Trek

The valley village sits around 3,050m; the Charakusa and K7 base-camp treks run 10-16 days door to door, reaching base camps around 4,000-4,500m. Moderate trekking in a technical-climbing arena.

  • Hushe, eastern Baltistan
  • Village ~3,050m
  • Day walks to ~10+ day treks
  • Easy to demanding

What's usually included - and what's not

On most of these treks the operator price includes guides and support staff, porters, camping equipment, in-trek meals and the trekking permit. It usually excludes international flights, travel insurance, personal climbing or trekking gear, tips for the crew, and on restricted peaks the government royalty fee. Always get the itemised inclusions, porter weight limit and the payment and cancellation terms in writing before you pay.

How to choose your operator

  • Published vs quote - a real posted price is easier to compare; for quote-only operators, get it itemised.
  • What's bundled - permits, domestic flights and porters are where cheap quotes cut corners.
  • Group size - most headline rates assume a group; confirm the solo or small-group rate.
  • Crew welfare - porter weight limits, wages and insurance say a lot about an operator.
  • Local vs down-country - Baltistan and Hunza-based crews know the routes and logistics best.

FAQ

How much does the Charakusa / Hushe trek cost in 2026?

Published prices run about $1,400-$2,800 per person depending on length and inclusions; several operators are quote-only.

What is there to trek to?

The Charakusa valley and K6/K7 base camps, plus approaches to Laila Peak, Masherbrum and the Gondogoro La - all from Hushe.

How hard is it?

Moderate trekking, though the setting is a serious climbing area; the base-camp walks themselves are non-technical.

When is the season?

June to September.
