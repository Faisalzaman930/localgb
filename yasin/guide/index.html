Overview

Yasin in
Plain Terms

Yasin is a long valley running north from Gupis in Ghizer District. The Yasin River flows through it from the glaciers above Darkot Pass, draining into the Ghizer River near Gupis. The valley is 120km in length and ranges from 2,200m at the main settlement to over 4,870m at Thui Pass in the upper reaches.

Yasin town — the main bazaar — is the administrative and commercial hub. It has a PTDC rest house, a small bazaar, a hospital, and basic services. Everything beyond Yasin town becomes increasingly remote and poorly connected.

Culturally, Yasin is distinctive within GB: the population is predominantly Shia Muslim rather than Ismaili, and the primary language is Khowar (also called Chitrali), which links the valley culturally to Chitral more than to Hunza or Gilgit. The area has a long documented history — rock engravings near the town predate Islamic settlement by centuries.

Important: No ATMs exist in Yasin. The nearest ATM is in Gahkuch, approximately 100km back toward Gilgit. Carry sufficient cash before leaving Gilgit.

Transport

Getting to
Yasin Valley

From Gilgit (shared)NATCO bus Gilgit→Gupis daily from General Bus Stand, Rs.400 (4h). Then shared Suzuki or pickup from Gupis bazaar to Yasin, Rs.300–500 (3h). Total Rs.700–900, 6–7h.
From Gilgit (private)Private jeep or 4WD: Rs.12,000–18,000 return. Travel directly without transfers. 5–6h one-way. Book through agencies in Gilgit or ask at your hotel.
Road conditionsPaved KKH to Gahkuch. Then paved but narrower road via Gupis. Yasin Valley road: partly paved, some rough sections. Sedan can manage to Yasin town in dry weather.
Key waypointsGilgit → Gahkuch (120km, 2h) → Gupis (70km, 1.5h) → Yasin town (30km, 45min).
Accommodation

Where to
Stay

PTDC MotelYasin town. Rs.2,000–3,500/room. Basic but maintained. Meals available if arranged in advance. The most reliable option.
Local guesthousesSeveral informal options in and near the bazaar. Rs.800–1,500/room. Ask at the bazaar on arrival — people will direct you. Shared bathrooms. Cold water only in most.
CampingPossible almost anywhere in the valley with local permission. Usually given freely. Bring full camping kit as backup.
Upper valleyNo formal accommodation above Yasin town. Camping only, or ask for homestays in Immit village.

What to See

Thui Pass4,870m. Connects to Punial/Ghizer. Multi-day trek requiring guide and camping kit.

PetroglyphsPre-Islamic rock carvings near Yasin town. Ask locally for exact location.
Darkot GlacierApproach walkable from upper valley. No guide needed for the approach — guide essential for the pass itself.
Food & Daily Life

Eating in
Yasin Valley

Yasin is not a food destination. The valley's hospitality is genuine but the cuisine is subsistence-level simple: dal and chapati at almost every meal, supplemented by rice when available. In season (July–October), local apricots and apples are outstanding — small, intensely flavoured varieties that don't exist outside the valley.

The bazaar in Yasin town stocks basic provisions: eggs, tinned goods, biscuits, cooking oil, lentils, dried fruit. There are small tea stalls where you can get chai and sometimes fried eggs. For anything more substantial, your guesthouse will cook on request — usually dal, chapati, and perhaps a vegetable dish. Give them an hour's notice.

Water: Yasin River water is glacial and fast-moving upstream, but do not drink valley floor water directly. Bring water purification tablets or a filter, or boil water at your guesthouse.
