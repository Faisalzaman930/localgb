Is Gilgit-Baltistan Open for Tourists in 2026?

By Faisal Zaman·Local, Gilgit-Baltistan·24 June 2026·Updated October 2026

One of the most-asked questions every summer, so here's the straight answer for 2026.

Yes - Gilgit-Baltistan is open to tourists, including foreign travellers, and it is consistently rated among the safest parts of Pakistan to visit. Hunza in particular is a favourite for women and solo travellers.

Updated October 2026: still open. After unrest in early March 2026 (curfews in Gilgit and Skardu and a mobile shutdown), the UK FCDO advised against all but essential travel to GB from 12 March; it lifted that on 15 April 2026, and GB is not under any FCDO "advise against" warning as of October 2026. The FCDO still advises against all travel on the KKH between Mansehra and Chilas. The US rates Pakistan as a whole Level 3 "Reconsider Travel", with GB not in its Level 4 areas.

What "open" means in practice

  • Towns and main valleys (Hunza, Skardu, Gilgit, Karimabad) are open and reachable year-round.
  • High passes are seasonal: Babusar roughly late May/June to October/November, Deosai roughly mid-June to late September/early October. Khunjerab is officially open year-round for trade, but the practical tourist window is May-October.
  • Permits: most areas need no special permit; a few border-adjacent zones do. Foreign nationals must register at police checkposts, so carry several passport and visa copies.
  • Visa: the free "visa prior to arrival" was suspended from January 2026, so apply for a paid e-visa at visa.nadra.gov.pk 4-6 weeks ahead.

The practical risks are mostly weather and seasonal road access - late July-mid August floods closed major roads for days in both 2025 and 2026 - plus occasional unrest, when mobile data can be cut, so carry offline maps and cash. Check the current road & pass status before you finalise dates, and see tour packages for trips timed to the open season.
