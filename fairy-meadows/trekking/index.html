The Trek

Fairy Meadows
to Beyal Camp

The standard day hike from Fairy Meadows goes to Beyal Camp (4,200m), the traditional resting point for climbing expeditions heading up Nanga Parbat's Raikot Face. From the meadows you enter a dense pine forest almost immediately, then emerge after an hour into open rocky terrain as you cross a ridge. From there, the Raikot Glacier comes into view and Nanga Parbat's north face occupies the entire visual field.

I've done this hike a dozen times, at different seasons and with people of different fitness levels. The honest assessment: anyone in reasonable shape can do the return trip in a day. Children from about 10 years old manage it. The altitude is the variable — if you're coming from Islamabad (500m) to Fairy Meadows (3,300m) and then trying to hike to Beyal (4,200m) on your first day, you're asking for a headache at minimum. Spend a night at the meadows first.

No guide required, but: The trail from the meadows to the first ridge is clear. Above the ridge toward Beyal, the path becomes less obvious in places. In mist or low cloud, it's easy to lose. A local guide (Rs.1,500–2,500) is worth hiring if you're going beyond the ridge alone.

Beyond Beyal

Further
Options

Experienced hikers can continue from Beyal toward Nanga Parbat Base Camp (Raikot Base Camp) at approximately 4,100m — technically at the base of the mountain's climbing routes. This requires 4–5 days total from Fairy Meadows, camping equipment, and ideally a local guide. No permit required.

The longer Nanga Parbat Base Camp trek combines the Raikot approach (from Fairy Meadows) or the Rupal approach (from Astore Valley on the south side). The Rupal Face trek is more remote and rewarding for those who have done Fairy Meadows already.
