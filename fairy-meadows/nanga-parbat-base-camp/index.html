The Mountain

Killer Mountain
Up Close

Nanga Parbat (8,126m) is the 9th highest mountain on Earth and earned its reputation as the "Killer Mountain" honestly — 31 climbers died attempting it before Austrian Hermann Buhl reached the summit in 1953. He did it alone, without supplemental oxygen, in a 41-hour push from Camp V. He descended in the dark, stood on a ledge for the night at 8,000m, and walked back to camp the next morning. It remains one of mountaineering's most astonishing individual achievements.

The Raikot Face (the north face, visible from Fairy Meadows) rises approximately 7,000m from the valley floor — one of the largest vertical relief distances anywhere on the planet. The base camp for Raikot Face routes sits at approximately 4,100m at the edge of the glacier.

You don't need a permit to reach it. The trekking zone extends to 5,000m without permit requirements. What you do need is proper equipment, at least 4–5 days, and ideally a local guide for the upper section.

Trek Details

Day by Day
Itinerary

Day 1Arrive Fairy Meadows (3,300m). Rest. Acclimatise. Explore the meadow. Evening at guesthouse.
Day 2Day hike to Beyal Camp (4,200m). 5–6km, 3–4hrs. Return to Fairy Meadows for night.
Day 3Fairy Meadows → Beyal Camp. Camp overnight at Beyal (4,200m). Full camping equipment required.
Day 4Beyal → Nanga Parbat Raikot Base Camp (4,100m). 4–5km across moraine. Spectacular Raikot Face views. Camp at BC.
Day 5Explore base camp area. Optional: push toward Camp I route (technical, not recommended without guide). Return to Beyal or Fairy Meadows.
Day 6Return Fairy Meadows → Tato → Raikot Bridge → Gilgit.
Altitude warning: Raikot BC is at 4,100m. Many visitors come from Gilgit (1,500m) via Fairy Meadows (3,300m) — that's a 2,600m gain in 2 days. This is faster than recommended. Spending 2 nights at Fairy Meadows before ascending to BC significantly reduces altitude sickness risk.
